📋 Featured Case Study

HealthHub — Remote Employee
Healthcare Platform

A product thinking exercise exploring how remote-first teams access and manage healthcare benefits.

🎯
The Problem
Remote employees across different countries struggled to access, understand, and use their healthcare benefits. Fragmented coverage, unclear processes, and lack of local support led to low utilization and employee frustration.
✅
The Solution
HealthHub centralizes benefits management in one platform — giving remote employees a unified view of their coverage, a way to find local providers, and in-app guidance to navigate claims and appointments without friction.
👤
My Role
As a Product Support Specialist embedded in the product process, I defined user personas, mapped friction points, contributed to feature requirements, and proposed product improvements based on real support patterns.
🖥️
Platform Preview
A unified dashboard for remote employees to track benefits, find providers, and manage claims — built to eliminate confusion and reduce support tickets.
healthhub.app/dashboard
Target Users
Remote employees at globally distributed companies who lack HR proximity; mid-to-senior professionals managing personal and family healthcare across borders.
SJ
Sarah Johnson
Remote Marketing Manager, 34 — Based in Lagos, employed by a UK firm
Wants clear benefits overview Needs local provider access Frustrated by slow claims Values fast support Uses mobile-first
⚙️
Key Features
  • 🗺️ Unified benefits dashboard with coverage breakdown
  • 📍 Local provider directory with real-time availability
  • 📋 In-app claim submission & status tracking
  • 💬 Live support chat with escalation to human agents
  • 🔔 Smart notifications for renewals and claim updates
🔄
Product Improvements I Contributed
  • 📉 Identified claim-step drop-off → simplified 5-step flow to 2
  • 📖 Proposed onboarding checklist → reduced first-week support tickets
  • 🌍 Flagged language gap → team added multi-region content
  • 📊 Created feedback loop with Product team using CSAT + ticket trends
💡
What I Learned
This exercise reinforced that the best product improvements often come from support data — not assumptions. When I mapped support ticket themes to specific product screens, patterns emerged that engineering didn't have visibility into. Being in that middle space — understanding both the user's language and the product's architecture — is where I add the most value.
